# THE REBEL GRID — Coaster requirements and build acceptance

**Owner revision:** 2026-10-27. Companion to [REBEL_GRID_MASTERPLAN.md](REBEL_GRID_MASTERPLAN.md). **Status: DESIGN SPECIFICATION, not installed rides.** The native model, seating, special track pieces, DLC entitlement and completed operation must be verified individually on Raven before a concept is called buildable or complete.

The owner's top priority is **connected, logical pathways and queues**. More disconnected rides are not progress. Do not add a new attraction until the current attraction passes the access and operating checks below.

## Preserve the distinctions in the request

- The desired Maverick experience and a true stand-up coaster are separate requirements. Keep a low, aggressive seated multi-launch design **and** a genuine stand-up design rather than silently replacing either. Cedar Point's official Maverick description identifies its two launches and 95-degree first drop [S1].
- A flying coaster and a genuinely back-lying/supine coaster are not interchangeable. A brief inverted moment is not a back-lying train design.
- The X2-style controlled-rotation experience and the requested free-flipping experience are separate. S&S describes additional rails controlling its 4th Dimension seats; its Free Spin product instead uses magnetic influences with spin affected by loading and rider factors [S4, S5]. Do not label a programmed rotation as free spinning.
- The launch-shuttle must actually return backward to its station, brake, unload and redispatch. A backwards-looking car or an ordinary complete circuit does not satisfy it.
- Preserve the requested sensations rather than arguing over an uncertain historical Six Flags ride name. Do not claim the owner's Georgia example is a specific ride without confirmation. Keep LIM versus LSM propulsion descriptions accurate for whichever native PC2 model is selected.

### Additional concepts after the required roster is operational

**PACKET RACE:** two synchronized, separately safe red/blue circuits using different but closely timed routes through the same grid plaza. Synchronization and station capacity must hold over repeated dispatches; a single successful timed pass is not enough.

**DEADLOCK:** a true tilt-track coaster that holds above a black shaft before its track physically tilts and releases. A steep static drop is not a tilt mechanism. Use the native tilt element and verify train length and reset behaviour.

**STACK OVERFLOW:** a compact switch/drop/reverse coaster or dark-ride hybrid with one readable system-failure sequence. Use only the selected native chassis' supported mechanisms; do not combine incompatible track systems in a fictional description.

**MOUSE.EXE:** a smaller independent-car hairpin coaster as a palate cleanser between the giant rides, using tight black server aisles and sharp red/blue direction markers. The purpose is variety rather than another tall skyline object.

**FETCHIN' CLANKA: SYSTEM RECOVERY:** family-scale tracked ride through service bays, inspection arms and a malfunctioning data center. It uses a supported ride system with original scenery, not an invented custom ride engine.

These additions are deliberately different experiences. The novelty comes from layout, sequence, scale, architecture and motion the game can actually perform.

## Visual contract: no tropical park wearing a few neon signs

### Surfaces and landscape

The finished editable guest areas should read as an engineered city: black paving, charcoal terrain, metal decks, dark water, black foundations and large modular roofs. Remove or replace the stock palms, flower beds, hedges, grass gaps and Viking/tropical decoration inside each completed district.

Use glossy or reflective native finishes where available and verified. Do not imply that painted terrain becomes mirror-like. Large dark surfaces with red/blue light strips and controlled white reflections are preferable to thousands of tiny shiny tiles. Preserve native walkable paths under the visual treatment; scenery alone is not a path.

For areas outside the editable park boundary, use deliberate skyline screening where possible. Do not claim distant mountains or uneditable terrain have been changed. An unfinished grassy district remains **unfinished**, even if the first plaza is fully themed.

### Palette and contrast

- Obsidian / charcoal: `#090B10` for the dominant mass.
- Midnight navy: `#061A33` for secondary structures.
- Signal red: `#E10616` for one network/ride family.
- Saturated electric blue: `#244CFF` for the opposing family. Do not drift to teal/cyan.
- White: `#FFFFFF` for readable RT marks, wayfinding and functional path lighting.
- Steel gray: restrained supports, joints, service doors and mechanical details.

The Ole Miss influence is the navy/red/white identity and confident massing, not copying unrelated university marks. The park remains Rebel Tech branded.

### RT mark rules

Use the verified original mark at `assets/branding/grid-v1/rt-circle-original.png`; preserve its silhouette, proportions and spacing. Do not replace it with two typed letters or an approximate circle. The original was visually inspected during this work session.

Plan three contrast treatments: white RT mark on black/navy; red-accent RT treatment on white or pale steel; blue-accent RT treatment on white or pale steel. Preserve readable white outlines/panels where needed rather than putting low-contrast red text directly over blue.

Apply the identity to the entry gateway, station fascia, queue-entry pylons, train/scenery panels where supported, food and retail counters, restroom signs, route markers and plaza centerpieces. Alternate large hero applications with smaller repeated marks; not every sign should contain the entire detailed hero illustration.

Native PC2 UserMedia billboards remain the first supported route for original logo images. A file staged in UserMedia is not yet a placed, saved and reloaded sign. External Blender scenery is a separate pipeline, and an exported GLB cannot create a new train type or ride mechanic.

## Logical circulation, not a maze of decorative lines

Organize the finished park around a connected main loop with short district spurs. Keep the existing entrance functional during expansion. Reserve room for wider primary arteries and holding plazas around the high-capacity rides; choose actual widths after checking the native tool and observed guest flow rather than applying unsupported numerical settings.

Each attraction should have:

1. A clearly labeled queue mouth off a plaza, not on a blind tight corner or across the main boulevard.
2. One continuous native queue ending at the actual ride entrance gate.
3. A distinct native guest-path exit that returns to the circulation network downstream from the queue mouth.
4. Room for staff and maintenance access without putting shops or decorative props over a gate attachment.
5. Readable white exit/wayfinding signage; red and blue are visual identity, not the only indication of direction.

Avoid long dead ends and isolated plazas. Before surrounding a coaster with scenery, trace its entry and exit all the way to the park entrance. Use the actual gate markers, not decorative arches or operator booths. Remove confirmed orphan queue fragments rather than leaving them to look like connections.

## Mandatory per-ride acceptance gate

A ride progresses through **SPECIFIED → MODEL_VERIFIED → TRACK_TESTED → ACCESS_CONNECTED → GUEST_TESTED → SAVE_RELOAD_VERIFIED → THEMED**. Never collapse these into an ambiguous 'complete'.

- **Model verified:** native catalogue name, seating posture, correct rotation axis, required track elements and DLC status recorded. Any unsupported request stays explicitly blocked.
- **Track tested:** complete successful test cycles; inspect the full train, all brakes, launches, switches, reversing sections and station return. Review the game's force and excitement/fear/nausea results; a finished-looking circuit is not sufficient.
- **Access connected:** entrance queue and normal exit path connect to the park network. No relevant inaccessible entrance or park-return exit warning remains.
- **Guest tested:** staff assigned, real guests reach the queue, board, complete the ride and leave through the exit. Record the served-guest counter and screenshots. OPEN alone is insufficient.
- **Saved and reloaded:** create a short, distinct checkpoint name, wait for the file to finish writing, copy it to ignored D: backups, compare SHA-256, and test a subsequent load. Preserve earlier checkpoints.
- **Themed:** remove guest-visible grass/default decoration, install proper RT marks and native finishes, inspect daytime and night lighting, and recheck path/ride clearances after scenery placement.
- **Performance checked:** compare the same camera and simulation conditions before/after the district; reduce repeated small pieces and unnecessary lights if responsiveness degrades. Do not claim an FPS target without measuring it.

Do not silence warnings, disable guest requirements, or deliberately remove game safety checks merely to obtain a passing status. Existing sandbox power-off configuration must be disclosed separately from a genuinely connected utility network.
